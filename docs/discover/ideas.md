# Idea list (Discover step 3, unranked)

1. Papers, Please inverted: the visitor sits in the inspector's booth, and every company wanting their data slides documents under the glass.
2. Mesopotamian clay bulla: the receipt is a sealed clay envelope with the record baked inside, so checking it means breaking it.
3. Coat check: you hand over one thing, you get a numbered stub, and the stub is the only power that matters.
4. Split tally stick: notched and split in two, so visitor and requester each keep a half that only matches the other.
5. Coast Salish witnessing: witnesses are named and paid to remember, so a record is held by people rather than stored by a system.
6. Safe-deposit vault: two keys turn at once, and the bank's key does nothing without yours.
7. Cell membrane: a living semi-permeable wall deciding what crosses, at microscope scale, no interface chrome.
8. Hanko and vermilion ink: consent is a physical red stamp pressed slightly crooked, because a real hand did it.
9. Evidence bag: data in tamper-evident bags with a chain-of-custody form signed at each handoff.
10. Pawn shop: your life in the window on little tags, priced by you, nobody touches anything without asking.
11. Ransom note: the stolen identity told in cut-up letters, and the page is the demand letter sent back.
12. Nightclub bouncer: a bored doorman who has already said no to Google tonight.
13. Sin-eater: a company that openly absorbs the awkward, ugly part of handling your data.
14. Toe tag: every data point leaving your life gets a tag with name, time and who signed it out, in a county coroner's flat tone.
15. Wedding objection: the silent room waits, and nothing proceeds until the visitor actually speaks.
16. Confessional screen: a lattice between you and whoever is asking, so they hear only the narrow thing you chose to say.
